# verifiable.pro design system

This document describes the stylesheets in `static/site.css` (the site) and `static/chat.css` (the site assistant), the scripts that enhance the pages, the home page hero, the logo files in `static/` and the rules behind them. The site is a bilingual (English and Simplified Chinese) editorial publication about verifiable AI systems that starts from trusted execution environments (TEEs): news briefs, analysis, explainers, guides, a TEE hub, the verification stack and its trust tiers, a resource library and a glossary.

The stylesheets hold the values. This document names the tokens and components and explains what each is for, so it stays true while sizes and colours are tuned; check a value in the stylesheet before relying on it.

## Concept

### The home intro states the site's aim and points at the seven layers (26 October 2026)

The owner found that the intro above the hero said neither what the site is for nor how it relates to the drawing, and asked for more direct words. People, and the agents acting for them, need to know whether the AI system they talk to protects them, and the site reports and analyzes the work toward AI that can be checked at all seven layers drawn.

- **The three lines.** The kicker names the reader's concern: "Where your data goes and what protects it" (你的数据流向哪里、受到什么保护). The tagline states the site's view beside the plates it describes: "Verifiable AI needs evidence at all seven layers" (可验证 AI，七个层级都需要证据). The one-sentence lead says what the site does at those layers: "We report and analyze that evidence at each layer shown here, so you and your agents can check it." It refers to the drawing as "shown here" (图中), because the drawing stands beside the intro on wide screens and below it on phones.
- **The first link.** The tagline no longer spells out trusted execution environments, so the first link does: "Why we start from trusted execution environments (TEEs)" (我们为什么从可信执行环境（TEE）出发). In the 21rem column from 1184px, and on phones, it takes two lines.
- **The social cards.** The site's cards carry the new kicker and tagline. `tools/og_images.py` also accepts a Chinese font that Chrome reports by its localized name, such as 蘋方-簡 for PingFang SC. Twenty indexable pages share the site's card, whose alt text repeats the kicker and tagline, so the IndexNow fingerprint now leaves that alt text out: a change of kicker or tagline notifies the two home pages, not all twenty.

### Muse and Grok among the hero's examples (25 October 2026)

The owner asked that the Agents label show Muse, the personal agent Meta launched in September 2026, with its own mark in place of the Meta AI mark, that the Models label name Meta's newest model series, Muse Wonder, in place of Llama, and that it add xAI's Grok. Neither icon set carries the Muse mark, so it comes from the public-domain text logo on Wikimedia Commons ([Third-party notices](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md#wikimedia-commons)); Grok's comes from Lobe Icons. A label shows at most seven examples (`MAX_HERO_EXAMPLES` in `sitegen/content.py`), which keeps it on one line from 45em, so Hugging Face left the Models label to make room for Grok. The Meta AI and Hugging Face marks stay in `sitegen/logos.py`.

### Photos as lead images (24 October 2026)

The owner found the drawn lead illustrations weaker than Audit Commons' pictures and asked for real photographs, or pictures in that soft editorial style. The site has no image model, so news briefs now open with real photographs from sources whose licenses allow reuse, all cut to one 3:2 frame and given one colour grade, so that the set reads as one series beside the site's grays. This note supersedes "The illustrations' style" in the 23 October note below for news briefs. An illustration remains possible for any article, and the places a picture appears, its frame and mat and the decorative list copies stay as that note describes. The content guide's [Photos](CONTENT_GUIDE.md#photos) gives the sources, the licenses, the captions and the steps.

- **The grade.** `tools/photos.py` gives every photo the grade `soft-1`: saturation down to 88% and contrast down to 92% around middle gray (the CSS filter `saturate(0.88) contrast(0.92)`), then a faint warm tone (red × 1.012, blue × 0.976), so whites turn a warm off-white (`#f8f5ef`) and blacks lift to `#0a0a0a`. The photos come out softer and more muted than a camera's own, closer to the reference's pictures and to the site's grays and warm cinnabar. The grade never changes what a photo shows: there is no retouching, sharpening or local edit. A new grade gets a new id (`content.PHOTO_GRADES`), and a photo keeps the id it was made with until it is made again.
- **Four files per photo.** The photo (`<slug>.webp`, 1600 × 1067), copies of the same crop at 800 × 533 (`<slug>-800.webp`) and 400 × 267 (`<slug>-400.webp`), and a 1200 × 630 copy for link previews (`<slug>-og.jpg`), each without metadata and under about 250, 70, 25 and 150 KB. Every `<img>` of a photo offers the 400, 800 and 1600 px files in `srcset`, with `sizes` from `render.THUMB_SIZES` (each list copy's widest width at each breakpoint of section 22.3) or `render.FIGURE_SIZES` (the reading column), so a browser loads the smallest file wide enough for the picture on its screen. The rail's and the TEE rows' copies, 86 to 136 CSS px wide, take the 400 px file up to about three device pixels per CSS pixel, the lead brief and the wider list columns the 800 px file, and the article's figure the 1600 px file on sharp screens; `src` names the 800 px file in lists and the 1600 px file in the figure. Every `<img>` keeps its width and height, the figure loads eagerly and list copies load lazily, as before.
- **Link previews.** An article with a photo names the photo's 1200 × 630 copy in `og:image` and `twitter:image`, with the photo's alt text, and as the structured data's `image`, with the photo's credit and license address. Other articles keep their topic's social card.
- **The credit line.** Under a photo it reads "Photo: <credit> · <license> · Source", with the license and the source linked, then "Cropped and color-adjusted" (or "Color-adjusted" when the crop keeps the whole source) where the license asks that changes be indicated, as CC BY and CC BY-SA do. An illustration keeps its own credit line.
- **Dark pages.** A photo dims less than an illustration: on `[data-image-kind="photo"]`, `--media-dim` takes `--media-dim-photo` (`brightness(0.9)` in the dark themes), since a photo's tones are mid-range and the grade has softened them already. Forced colours and print show the pictures undimmed, as before.
- **What stays banned for photos:** AI-generated images, renders and composites of different scenes or subjects presented as photographs (a focus stack of a single subject counts as a photograph); retouching, local edits and filters beyond the one grade; watermarks and borders; a logo or an advertisement as the subject; an identifiable private person as the subject; and licenses outside the content guide's list.

### Choosing a layer by its name or its plate (24 October 2026)

After the logos gained their names (the note below), the owner found that pointing at them still opened the big layer cards, and asked that a defined area open a card, that the card stay open as the pointer moves onto it, and that each layer's name carry its code. This note supersedes what the notes below say about pointing at a label; *Opening a layer, from 45em* under [The hero](#the-hero) has the details.

- **The name area opens a layer.** A label's head line, with its icon, code and name, is the name link's target. It spans the label column from the row's rule and is at least 24px tall, and under the mouse the cursor becomes a pointer and the name is underlined. Pointing at it, or at the layer's plate in the scene, opens the layer and checks it, as before. Pointing at the examples under the name, its vendor marks and names, changes no layer: a mark only shows its brand name.
- **The card stays while the reader uses it.** An open card stays while the pointer is on its layer's name, its plate or the card, and while the pointer heads for the card. A name or a plate on the way waits instead of opening until the pointer rests on it for 0.2s or turns away, so the pointer can cross the gap, the examples and even the next label to reach a card and click its link. The card closes 0.3s after the pointer has left its name, its plate and the card; Esc closes it at once, and so does choosing another layer's name or plate. The card is thus hoverable, persistent and dismissible (WCAG 1.4.13).
- **Codes before the names.** Each label reads "T1 Hardware" to "T7 Policy" (T1 硬件 to T7 政策). The code is a quiet prefix, set in `--ink-3` and medium weight as the story tags set codes, and it takes the name's colour while the name is lit. Screen readers hear "T1, Hardware" (T1，硬件); the cards' heads, which show the code in a chip, read the same way.

### Less text on the home page, and the names of the hero's logos (24 October 2026)

The owner found the home page too wordy, naming the hero's caption, and asked that resting the pointer on one of the hero's small logos show that brand's name. Each block on the home page now keeps what a first-time reader needs there and leaves the explanation to the page it links to. This note retires the hero's caption and the legend's note, which older notes below still mention; the hero's layout, its cards and the checking of its layers are unchanged.

- **What the home page leaves out.** The hero has no caption. What the caption explained (a conceptual illustration, the chain of evidence, the TEE layers, your data's path through the seven layers, and that the layers are positions in this site's own scheme) is in the scene's description for screen readers, and the Stack page explains the scheme to everyone. The note that the logos and names are examples, not endorsements, is read to screen readers right before the layer labels (`p.vh#hero-layers-note`); About says it in view. The positioning is one sentence, and the person's label, the hint and the legend's title keep one short line each; the legend no longer ends on a note that checking stops where the chain breaks, which the legend under the Stack page's table says. The TEE block lists the path's steps by kind, reading time and title, and what each step lets the reader do stays on Learn and the TEE hub. The guide cards under "Checks you can run" give each guide's title, step count and reading time; its dek stays on Learn's worksheet card and on the guide.
- **The logos' names.** A mouse or pen that rests on a vendor mark for 150ms shows the mark's name in a small tooltip, in the page's language (欧盟 for the European Union on Chinese pages, as the Chinese articles write it), and moving on along the row's marks shows each name at once. A layer's card never lies over its own label's marks, so every mark's name shows, on a fresh page and after any rest. On touch screens a tap on a mark shows its name, and the open card names the row's examples, also for the part of a mark that a tablet's chevron lies over. A pointer sweeping the labels shows no name, and since the note above, pointing at a mark opens no layer (see *The marks' names* under [The hero](#the-hero)).

### Lead illustrations and a quieter home page (23 October 2026)

The owner asked for two things: smaller type on the home page, and a picture with every news story, as [Audit Commons](https://auditcommons.org/) gives its briefs, made in the site's own style and by a documented, repeatable method. This note supersedes two rules of the TEE redesign below: "One image" (only the home page hero) and the bans on article images and on thumbnails in lists. The rest of the redesign holds, and [What stays banned](#what-stays-banned) lists what remains off. The rules are section 22 of `static/site.css`, which only adds rules to the sections before it.

- **Where the pictures go.** An article whose folder holds an `illustration.json` (see the content guide's [Lead images](CONTENT_GUIDE.md#lead-images)) has a lead image, and `content/site.json` `"lead_image_kinds"` lists the kinds that must have one: news. The image appears in six places and nowhere else:
  1. *The article page:* `figure.lead-figure`, right after the head (title, dek and meta line), as wide as the reading column (`--measure`), with the record column beside it from 1024px. The picture links to the full-size file and carries the spec's alt text; it loads eagerly with `fetchpriority="high"`. The caption follows, then a credit line in `--ink-3`: "Drawn by code from its published scene description." (the spec, linked) and "How we choose photos and make illustrations" (About's `#illustrations`); a photo's credit line names its credit, its license and its source, each linked.
  2. *The home page's lead brief:* the picture above the kicker and title, at most 32rem wide (28rem from 960 to 1279px, where the rail beside it is narrow). From 960px the rail beside it runs five briefs, so the two end about level; below 960px, under the brief, it shows four.
  3. *The home page's rail and the TEE block's latest rows:* a 6.5rem copy to the right of the title and meta line (5.5rem below 480px, so the title keeps a wider column; in the rail beside the lead brief, 5.5rem from 960px, where the rail is only about 21rem wide, and 7.5rem from 1280px).
  4. *Latest and the topic pages:* the list (`.river`) is a query container, so a row takes its shape from the list's width, not the screen's: a topic page's list shares the page with the record column, Latest's runs the full width. In a list narrower than 30rem (phones) the picture stands above the date, at most 15rem wide. From 30rem the date sits above the story, as on phones, and the picture takes a column at the right, 8 to 11rem wide (a topic page at every width, Latest from 512 to 1023px). From 60rem (Latest from 1024px) the row runs date, story and a 13.5rem picture, and from 68rem an 18rem picture, so the picture stays close to a dek that keeps its measure.
  5. *The TEE hub's coverage list:* the copy at the right, as in every other list, 8.5rem wide from 760px; the list keeps to the hub's 46rem measure, as its intros and responses do, so each copy stays beside its title.
  6. *Related cards:* the picture on top, only when all three cards have one, so the row keeps one shape. From 600 to 959px the cards run two to a row, and the third spans the row with its picture under the first card and its text under the second, so no card stands alone.
- **List copies are decorative.** Every copy sits beside a title link that already names the story, so it is `<img alt="">` inside `a.story-thumb` with `aria-hidden="true"` and `tabindex="-1"`: screen readers skip it and Tab passes over it, so each row keeps one tab stop. Pointing at a copy underlines the row's title. Copies load lazily and decode asynchronously, and every `<img>` carries its width and height, so nothing shifts as pictures arrive. Rows without a picture keep their text layout.
- **Frame, mat and themes.** A picture sits on `--media-mat` (the tint in light, the page in dark) inside a 1px `--media-frame` hairline, with no shadow and no rounding; list copies are cropped to the illustrations' 3:2 canvas (`--media-ratio`). Illustrations are files shown through `<img>`, so they keep their fixed light palette in both themes; on dark pages `--media-dim` (`brightness(0.78) contrast(0.92)`) brings their median brightness down to about that of the body text, so they do not read as light slabs, while the orange and the indigo stay distinct. In forced colours the frame draws in `CanvasText` and the dimming is off. Print keeps the article's figure, caption and credit (at most 11cm high) and leaves out the list copies.
- **The illustrations' style.** `tools/illustrate.py` draws each picture from its spec's `scene` in the hero's language: the same three-quarter projection and light from the upper left, gray plates and props, indigo only for TEE things and the chain of evidence, the reader's orange for data, cinnabar only for a seal, soft shadows, gradients only on plate tops and round solids, at most one glow (on a data path, an agent's core or a TEE die), and no words, digests or check marks inside the picture (see `tools/scene/README.md`).
- **A quieter home page.** On the home page display type (the tagline and the lead brief's title) steps down by about a sixth at every width, and running text by about 1px, keeping its order, with home-scoped rules (`.page-home`) in section 22. At 1440px: the tagline from 40 to 33px (Chinese 36 to 30px), the lead brief's title from 36 to 30px, the lead dek and the positioning from 18 and 17px to 16px, the rail's and the TEE block's titles from 17 to 16px, the guide cards' titles from 20 to 17px and their deks from 15 to 14px, section heads from 17 to 16px (Chinese 18 to 17px), the TEE block's column heads and the TEE path's step titles from 16 to 15px (the Chinese column heads stay on the label scale, at 13px, as before), the kicker from 14 to 13px and the two links from 15 to 14px. The tagline is 37px at 768px and 30px at 390px (it was 44 and 36px; Chinese 36 and 29px), and the lead brief's title 26px at 768px and 22px at 390px (it was 31 and 26px). Metadata stays at 13px, and the hero, its labels and its cards keep their sizes.

### TEE redesign (21 October 2026)

TEEs are now the site's main subject, and the design shows it in three ways: one muted indigo marks TEE coverage, every row of the verification stack says what a TEE contributes at that layer, and five trust tiers compare the other ways to check a claim with a TEE. This note supersedes two rules of the reading-first restyle below. "No generated pictures" gives way to exactly one image on the pages, the home page hero (the social cards that link previews show are pictures too, but no page shows them) (superseded on 23 October 2026, when articles gained lead images; see the note above), and "no layer codes" gives way to layer codes in plain sans on every listed story. The rest of the restyle still holds, and [What stays banned](#what-stays-banned) lists what remains off. The redesign's rules are section 19 of `static/site.css`, which only adds rules to the sections before it.

- **TEEs come first.** The header runs Vision, TEEs, Latest, Stack, Learn and Resources: Vision, the site's purpose, sits right beside the wordmark. "TEEs" (TEE 专题) opens the TEE hub at `/topics/confidential-computing/`, the existing address of that topic, so older links keep working. The site's positioning and goals are written once, on Vision (`/vision/`); the home page, About, the footer and the page metadata summarize it, and the first three link there.
- **One image.** *(Superseded on 23 October 2026: articles now carry lead images; see the note above.)* The pages carry exactly one image: the hero in the home page's first band, a drawn scene of the seven layers of an AI system and of you, the reader, talking with it from outside the stack, inlined as SVG, with a label and a layer card for each layer beside it (see [The hero](#the-hero)). The scene draws the chain of evidence as each binding holds today: solid, dashed or broken. `static/hero.js` opens the cards, counts the layers the reader has checked and, once all seven are, animates the scene. There are no plates, no section pictures, no article images and no thumbnails in lists, and the drawings generated from article digests stay off (`"illustrations": false`). Every other page is text, so spacing, type, rules, numbered rows and folds have to give it structure; a text page should read as designed, not as a wall of text. The social cards in `static/og/` (one for the site and one per topic, in each language, which `tools/og_images.py` draws with `sitegen/art.py`'s patterns) are the only other pictures the site publishes, besides the logo files and the icons drawn from the mark (see [Logo](#logo)): the pages name them in their `og:image` and `twitter:image` tags, so they appear only in link previews, and no page shows one.
- **The TEE colour.** `--tee-ink`, `--tee-wash` and `--tee-rule` are a muted indigo, the tint of the hero's two TEE layers. They mark TEE coverage and nothing else: links, buttons and focus stay cinnabar, which remains the only interaction colour. TEE text reaches at least 7:1 on its wash in both themes, and in forced colours the TEE label and the spine draw in `CanvasText`. The interface has no gradients and no glows.
- **Where the indigo goes.** The TEE label on a story; the TEE line in each stack row; the TEE tier, on the wash under a 3px rule; the 3px rule that closes the TEE hub's head; the "How this compares with a TEE" callout on the other topic pages; and, in the home page hero, the rule, icon and leader line of the two TEE layers' labels, the TEE line of each layer card, with the code chip and the top rule of the T1 and T2 cards, as well as the scene's own tint of those two plates, the ring on the person's phone, the chain's own mark, the reader's data packets while they pass the two TEE layers and the front edges of those two plates as the packets reach them, and the phone's screen and the shield over the person while T1 or T2 is open, and the samples of the line in the legend of the chain, drawn in the chain's indigo so that they match it. The legend's words and the person's label are in ink. A story carries the TEE label exactly when `confidential-computing` is among its topics.
- **Tags.** In lists, a story's meta line (date · first publisher) ends with its stack-layer codes in plain sans ("T2 T1") and, for a TEE story, one outlined "TEE" label. Tags are not links and add no tab stops. One note, under the Latest filters and under the Stack page's table, explains them.
- **The stack table and the TEE spine.** Seven rows run from T7 at the top to T1 at the bottom, and each row has a TEE line. At T1 and T2 the evidence is direct, so the line sits on the wash behind a 3px `--tee-ink` rule. Above them a TEE's evidence counts only through code it measures, so the rule fades to `--tee-rule` and the wash goes. Read from top to bottom, the faded rules run into the strong ones like a spine, and the reach label ("Direct evidence", "Only through measured code") says the same in words, so nothing depends on colour.
- **Trust tiers.** The Stack page compares the other approaches with a TEE: an order note, the provider's word as an unnumbered baseline, then five tiers ordered by what a passing check rests on, from a party's statement to hardware evidence to recomputation and proof. The note says that the order shows where the trust sits and is not a ranking. In Chinese, trust tiers are 档 and stack layers are 层.
- **Short pages.** Where data has many fields, a row shows a few short lines and folds the rest into `<details>`. A tier row shows what a passing check establishes and what it does not establish; platform cards, attacks and the sources of each tracker row fold their details. Pages open on short items, and readers unfold the rest.

#### What stays banned

The redesign brought back one image on the pages, one colour and the layer codes, not the receipts; since 23 October 2026 articles also carry lead images, in the places the note above lists. These stay off:

- digests in the reading flow (the folded verification record at the end of each article is the only place a page prints one);
- mono labels and small tracked capitals (Plex Mono is for code);
- seal squares, receipts, perforations and tombstones on the pages (the logo and the site assistant keep their own seal);
- dot grids, strata glyphs and layer locators;
- gradients, glows and check marks in the interface (the hero's scene paints its own soft shadows, the glow of its evidence line and the glow behind the person while a layer is open, and shows no check mark). The one mark allowed is the small tick beside a hero layer's name once the reader has opened that layer: it records what the reader has looked at in this page view, not the result of a check;
- people on the plates, a person drawn as a layer, and crowds; check marks on the person or in their moment (the hero's one person stands on the ground outside the stack; at rest the phone shows only the chain's ring, and it turns indigo with a lock only while T1 or T2 is open or the reader clicks the person);
- in lead illustrations: words, digests and check marks inside the picture, logos, real people or products, crowds, gradients other than the shading of plate tops and round solids, and more than one glow (on a data path, a glowing agent core or a TEE die) beside the soft shadows that the hero also paints;
- pictures anywhere else in lists (Learn, Resources, the glossary, search, the Stack page and the path lists stay text), the old square `.thumb` copies, and pictures without a published spec;
- shadows, rounded corners and hover zooms on pictures.

### Reading-first restyle (21 October 2026)

Readers found the receipts dense and the page machine-made, so a restyle earlier the same day put the reporting first. Two of its rules still hold everywhere:

- **Digests stay out of the reading flow.** Each article ends with "Check or correct this page": correction links and a closed "Verification record" holding the source digest, a copy button and links to the source and the manifest. The home page, lists, the footer and the assistant's answers show no digests.
- **Quieter type.** Labels are in sentence case in IBM Plex Sans, and Plex Mono is for code only. Hairlines are solid. There are no seal squares, topic swatches, strata locators or tombstones.

The restyle's front page (the lead story with two analysis pieces beside the five latest briefs) gave way to the redesign's home page. Its overrides live in the unnumbered "Restyle layer" between sections 18 and 19 of `static/site.css`.

### Earlier concept (19–20 October 2026)

The first design presented the site as a publication that shows its receipts: a definition band with the manifest digest on the front page, briefs filed "on the record" with receipt lines and strata locators, a claim-check card under the lead story, "Check our work" receipts, a mono layer for every machine-checkable value, and a seal-red square as the recurring sign. ## Colour

Every colour is a custom property defined on `:root`, in section 1 of `site.css` except the `--media-*` tokens of section 22. The dark theme redefines the same tokens under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) { :root:not([data-theme="light"]) }` and again under `:root[data-theme="dark"]`, so `data-theme` on `<html>` can force either theme. The header's light/dark toggle sets it: the page follows the system theme until the reader picks one, which `site.js` keeps in `localStorage` (`vp-theme`) and an inline script in the head applies before the first paint (see the header under [Components](#components)). `tools/shoot.py` uses the attribute for dark screenshots. The dark theme is designed, not inverted: surfaces step up from the page, the accent lightens, structural rules drop to a mid grey, the footer sinks below the page, the header mark's tile turns light, and the hero's scene switches to its own dark palette on the page ground.

Elements carry their topic as `data-topic` (or `data-group` on catalog groups), and links to a topic page are matched by their path; either sets `--topic` and `--swatch` for the element. Rules for colour:

- The accent is the only interaction colour. Do not introduce a second accent, and never use a `--tee-*` token in a link, a button or a `:focus` rule.
- Indigo marks TEE coverage only, in the places the redesign note lists.
- Components use tokens only. New components must not use a literal colour; the hero's SVG scene keeps its own paint, as `hv-` custom properties inside the scene with a light and a dark set, the person's included. Its labels, leader lines, layer cards, legend and pause control use the site's tokens.
- Text tokens pass WCAG AA against every ground they sit on, in both themes, and TEE text reaches 7:1 on its wash. Check `--ink-3` on `--tint` and `--tint-2` after changing any of them.

## Type

The page head links three Google families: Newsreader (optical sizes 6–72; 400, 500, 600; italic 400), IBM Plex Sans (400, 500, 600) and IBM Plex Mono (400, 500). The stylesheets use no `@import`, and no other weights are used, so there is no synthesized bold. Each family token (`--serif`, `--sans`, `--mono`) falls back to system faces; the restyle layer points `--mono` at the sans, so only code is set in Plex Mono.

| Role | Face | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headlines, deks, English reading text, the home tagline, stack claims, glossary terms, the wordmark | Newsreader | 500 for page and article titles and the tagline; 600 for list titles, cards, path and tier titles and the wordmark. Italic only for the `.pro` of the wordmark, stack claims, river months, quotations and emphasis |

| Code, including the digest in the verification record | IBM Plex Mono | Mixed case |

The scale is one set of custom properties, `--fs-1` to `--fs-10`: seven fixed steps for the interface and reading text, then three fluid `clamp()` steps for display headlines. `--fs-1` (12px) is the floor: nothing in the interface is smaller, and citation superscripts use `max(0.62em, 0.75rem)`. Line heights (`--lh-tight`, `--lh-head`, `--lh-ui`) and tracking (`--track-title`, `--track-head`, `--track-label`) are tokens as well, so the Chinese edition can redefine them.

Reading text:

- **English** prose is Newsreader. `--prose-size` steps up from `--fs-5` to `--fs-6` at 760px, and the measure holds about 60 to 70 characters per line.
- **One right edge.** `--measure` is a root-relative (`rem`) length, so paragraphs, headings, callouts, tables and sources share one right edge whatever their own size.
- Headings use `text-wrap: balance`, paragraphs `text-wrap: pretty`, and dates and counts use tabular figures.

### Chinese

Chinese pages (`[lang="zh-CN"]`) redefine the type tokens. The tokens sit on the `lang` attribute, so Chinese islands in English pages and English islands in Chinese pages pick up the right stacks.

- **Body and interface** put the CJK face first: PingFang SC, Hiragino Sans GB, Noto Sans CJK SC, Source Han Sans SC, Microsoft YaHei, then Plex Sans. Full-width punctuation therefore renders in the CJK font.
- **Headlines** keep Newsreader for Latin and fall back to Songti SC, Noto Serif CJK SC and Source Han Serif SC, then Microsoft YaHei, so Windows without CJK serif fonts sets them in a bold sans, never in faux-bold SimSun. Page and article titles and the home tagline are set at 600; the tagline breaks at its comma rather than inside a word.
- **Section heads** (`.block-title`, river months, prose `h2`), stack claims and the assistant's title use `--cjk-serif`, a stack that puts the CJK serif first, instead of small tracked capitals or italics.
- **Prose** is set in the CJK sans with looser leading, 0.02em tracking and a wider `--measure` of about 35 characters per line. The positioning paragraph, the TEE lines, tier fields and tracker cells take the same looser leading.
- **Typesetting rules:**
  - Headlines have no negative tracking, and all line heights are looser.
  - Labels, layer codes, tags and the TEE label are at least 13px (`--fs-2`).
  - `line-break: strict` and `text-autospace` are on.
  - `em` uses emphasis dots instead of italics.
  - Chinese text is never set in italic; the wordmark keeps its italic `.pro`, and an English answer in the assistant keeps its italics.
  - Analysis pieces have no drop capital.

### The hero

The figure `.hero-visual` in the home page's first band is the site's one drawn scene inlined in a page (articles carry lead images as files, and the social cards appear only in link previews): a drawn scene of the seven layers of an AI system and of you, the reader, talking with it from outside the stack, with a label and a layer card for each layer, the person's label, the legend of the chain, and two links to the Stack page; it has no caption. Its markup reads in the order a phone shows it: the pause control and the "Check all layers" button, which takes its place until every layer is checked, then the stage (the scene, the leader lines, the person's label with the count of layers checked, the hint, the note on the logos for screen readers, the layer rows and the legend), then the links, and last the tooltip that names a vendor mark.

- *The scene* is the inline SVG in `sitegen/templates/hero-scene.svg`, an exploded stack of seven plates in a 30° orthographic view: an accelerator board with a GPU die ringed by memory stacks and a CPU with a padlock (T1); an inference server in a glass enclave beside an attestation report (T2); a neural network and a dataset (T3); generated text and an image with a provenance tag (T4); agents calling tools (T5); benchmark bars and a gauge (T6); and a sealed agreement with standards documents and a pair of scales (T7). An indigo line, the chain of evidence, rises from the GPU die through a ring on every plate toward the seal, and the two TEE layers are tinted indigo. The scene carries no text: its `{{SCENE_TITLE}}` and `{{SCENE_DESC}}` are filled in the page language, and it keeps `role="img"`, named by the title (`aria-labelledby`) and described by the description (`aria-describedby`), so the name stays short. The description (`hero_scene_desc`) says what the still scene shows, which is all a page without the script has, so it names no gray; the description's `data-hv-live`, filled from `{{SCENE_DESC_LIVE}}` (`hero_scene_desc_live`), holds the sentences on the gray and the moving data, and `hero.js` adds them to the description once it has set the figure up, so the description is true with the script and without it. The figure has no caption, so the description also says what a caption said until 24 October 2026: that the scene is a conceptual illustration, that the indigo line is the chain of evidence and the two tinted plates are the layers where TEEs give direct evidence, that the dotted path carries your prompts and data and brings an answer back, and that the layers are positions in this site's own scheme, not security levels, to which the stories' T1 to T7 tags refer. It has no ground and no frame and sits on the page in both themes. Its colours are its own `hv-` custom properties, with a dark set under `prefers-color-scheme: dark` and `[data-theme="dark"]`, and every class, id, keyframe and custom property in it starts with `hv-`, except the root's class `hero-scene` and the ids `hero-scene-title` and `hero-scene-desc`, which the page refers to. Its inline `<style>` styles only `hv-` classes, so it cannot reach the page; it reads the figure's attributes (below) through attribute selectors.
- *The person* is you, the reader, talking with an AI system. They stand on the ground in front of the board's left corner, outside the stack and on the reader's side: one adult seen from behind, in a knee-length amber coat and blue trousers, bowing their head over a phone held in both hands. The phone's screen shows the chain's indigo ring, and its halo lights with the ring at the die. Drawn at 1.2 times its first size, the figure is about 135px tall at 1440 × 900 and 104px at 390, a fifth of the drawing's height. It is drawn in fills only (`hv-pcoat`, `hv-plegs`, `hv-phair` and the like), with no face, no outline and no platform; a soft shadow under the feet sets it on the ground. The coat, the trousers and your data take their colours from the page's tokens `--you-coat`, `--you-coat-2`, `--you-legs`, `--you-legs-2` and `--you-data`, set in all three theme blocks of `site.css` (light, dark by system and dark by choice), so the theme toggle and the system setting both reach them; the scene reads each with its own value for that theme as the fallback. In light the coat is a bright amber, `#df7a00` and `#c2620a` (3.0:1 and 4.2:1 on white), over clear blue trousers, `#2f6fd0` and `#2457a8`, and dark brown hair: brighter and more saturated than the dark set, so that you stand out on the pale plates. In dark the coat is `#e09a45` and `#c27f2e` over `#7e9cc7` trousers and `#85634f` hair. The coat, both trouser legs and the hair keep at least 3:1 on the page in both themes, and only the shoes, a detail on the foot shadow, stay below it. With the script you are drawn in gray until you have checked the layers (see *Checking the layers*). The amber is the person's own, warm against the indigo of the TEE layers, and no layer uses it. It is the scene's thesis drawn: the person whose prompts and data go into the system, and who, alone or through someone checking for them, can check the provider's claims, starting from the chip. The viewBox grew 28 units on the left for it (`-28 0 382 597`); nothing else moved, and the leader anchors keep their coordinates. `tools/hero/person.py`, with `tools/hero/geom.py` (standard library), draws it: it poses the figure in 3D, projects it into the scene's view, scaled by `SCALE` about the feet at `FEET` (now 1.2 about (-15, 557.5)), and bakes that size and place into the path data, which stays left of x = 7.3, 6 units clear of the board. After changing the figure or its placement, `python3 tools/hero/person.py --write` rewrites the person's block in the scene, from its comment line to its hit area; `--check`, which the unit tests run, fails when the scene is not what the script draws. Its docstring holds the review protocol (1x and 2x, light and dark, at 1440 and 390).
- *The person's moment* is the scene's small delight, drawn by `person.py` around the figure: a warm glow behind them (`hv-you-glow`), the phone's screen in the TEE's indigo with a small lock on it (`hv-pscreen-tee`, `hv-plock`), a thought bubble over their head with an indigo shield and a lock and two gold sparkles (`g.hv-bubble`), and two indigo ripples from the phone (`hv-ripple`). While any layer is open, you are lit: the glow fades in and the whole figure brightens, through one filter on `g.hv-person`, after the gray of the layers not yet checked (`--hv-lit`: in light a saturation lift, dimmed a little so that the bright coat keeps 3.1:1 on white, `saturate(1.3) brightness(.93)`; in dark a brightness and saturation lift, `brightness(1.12) saturate(1.2)`). While T1 or T2 is open, the layers where a TEE gives direct evidence, the screen turns indigo with its lock and the bubble rises over your head; with motion allowed, you give a little hop (3.4 units, `hv-hop`; the foot shadow stays on the ground) and the sparkles burst out once. The hop plays whole: `hero.js` sets `data-hv-hop` on the figure for its 0.7s when T1 or T2 opens from another layer or from none, and the scene keys the hop on that, so a pointer that moves on to T3 or leaves mid-hop does not drop you to the ground, and one that goes back and forth between T2 and T3 does not restart it while it plays. A click or a tap on the person plays the same moment for 1.8s (`data-hv-cheer` on the figure, `move`, or `still` when the reader has paused the motion), with the two ripples, and so does checking the last of the seven layers. The person's hit area (`path.hv-you-hit`, their box and a little more) is a pointer target only, from the script on (`data-enhanced`): the scene is an image (`role="img"`), so a control inside it would be lost to screen readers, the moment says nothing that the TEE rows do not say in words, and every reader, keyboard included, sees it by opening T1 or T2, so no tab stop is spent on it. The hop, the twinkle and the ripples are one-off answers to the reader's own act: they run while a card holds the loop (`data-motion="suspended"`), but not once the reader has paused the motion, and never under reduced motion, where the moment is a still change of state. The glow, the screen and the bubble ease in over 0.2 to 0.5s, and the bubble goes in 0.12s. Each rule names one of the moment's classes, and each part that shows or hides is one element (the screen and its lock are one group, the two sparkles one path, the figure is lit by one filter, and the data packets hide with their group's opacity), so opening a card restyles about nine more elements than before and a switch six, and the moment repaints only the person's corner of the scene. No check mark appears anywhere in it.
- *The data flow.* A dotted route (`path.hv-route`) carries your prompts and data from the phone through every layer in order, touching one object on each plate: along the board in front of its capacitors, across the dashed TEE boundary onto the GPU die (T1), up into the glass enclave onto the inference server (T2), out of the glass to the neural network (T3), the generated-text card (T4), the agent (T5), the tallest benchmark bar (T6) and the top sheet of the standards documents (T7). The answer comes back down the left of the stack, outside every plate, to the phone. Each leg is a smooth curve through the touch points and a few via points that keep it clear of the scene's objects, and the route stays in the scene, left of the labels and the cards. On the 12-second loop two small packets leave the phone at 0.15s in the coat's amber (`--you-data`), turn the TEE's indigo, with a soft ring, where they cross the TEE boundary on the board (`--hv-pkring`: white on light pages, so that a packet resting on the dark GPU die stays visible, and indigo on dark ones), and turn amber again where they leave the top of the glass; they rest 0.22s on each object and reach T7 at 7s, and an answer packet runs back to the phone from 7.15s to 9.8s. As a packet reaches a layer, that plate's front edges (`path.hv-reach`) light in the layer's hue, indigo on T1 and T2, for under a second, from 1.7s at T1 to 7s at T7. The phone's halo lights as the data leaves and again as the answer arrives, together with the ring at the die; then the pulse rises (9.8s to 11.85s), so the data and the pulse never move at once, and the scene is still until the loop starts again. `tools/hero/flow.py` computes the route from the scene's geometry (the ring on the phone from `person.py`, the TEE boundary `hv-tb`, the glass's top `hv-gt` and each layer's object) and writes the markup in two groups: `g.hv-flow-lo`, before the glass's front faces, holds the way from the phone to the top of the glass, so that a packet inside the glass is seen through it, and `g.hv-flow-hi`, over the top plate and under the person, holds the rest and the lit edges; each request packet is a circle in each group, and the two hand over at the top of the glass. Between two comments in the scene's style it writes the whole loop: the rules and keyframes of the pulse (`hv-rise`), the rings and the phone's halo (`hv-ping`, `hv-yping`, and each ring's `--hv-d`), the packets (`hv-q`, `hv-r`, `hv-a`) and the edges (`hv-reached`). Each packet is a circle moved by a CSS transform along keyframes 50ms apart, eased at both ends of each leg; `--check`, which the unit tests run, fails when the scene is not what it draws. The packets and the edges loop with the pulse: they run only under `data-animated`, hold while it is paused or suspended, stop under `data-motion="static"`, are hidden while a layer is open, and are not drawn under reduced motion, where the dotted route alone says where your data goes. They repaint only a few pixels a frame.
- *The chain at rest* is drawn as each binding holds today, from `stack[].bind.status`: solid where the binding to the layer below is in use (T1 to T2 today), dashed where it is emerging (up to T6's ring), and, where it is missing, two stubs and a gap (under the seal). The pulse fades at the first missing link, and the rings above it do not ping. The person stands beside the solid part, so nothing cuts them off from the chip. This is owner decision 1 (see [Owner decisions](#owner-decisions)); with `HERO_CHAIN_REST = "whole"` the chain is drawn unbroken at rest and shows the statuses only while a layer is open.
- *Groups and hit areas.* The scene's elements are wrapped in place, without reordering, in groups the figure's attributes reach: `g.hv-layer.hv-ly1` to `hv-ly7` (each plate and its objects, some layers in two runs), `g.hv-seg.hv-seg2` to `hv-seg7` (segment k, layer k's binding to the layer below, from ring k − 1 up to ring k, with its two stubs) and `g.hv-ring.hv-ring1` to `hv-ring6`; the cast shadows carry `hv-cast`. The last element is `g.hv-hits`, seven transparent paths (`path.hv-hit[data-hv-n]`, T1 first so an upper plate wins where two overlap), each a plate's top face and front thickness and the objects that rise above it; they take no pointer events until the figure has `data-hv-hits`. The person's own hit area, `path.hv-you-hit`, stands before `g.hv-hits`, left of every plate's, and plays their moment; it opens no layer. `build.py`'s `hero_scene_check` stops a build whose scene has lost any of this: the hit areas in order, a layer, segment or ring group, a segment's two stubs, or the person with the ring on their phone. It also stops a build that puts a transform on the person or on anything in it, as an attribute or in a `style`: the person's geometry belongs in its path data, where the tests read its bounds. The hop is the scene stylesheet's animation of `g.hv-person`, which ends where it starts.

- *Opening a layer, from 45em.* One layer is open at a time. A layer's triggers, for a mouse or pen, are its plate in the scene and its label's name area (its head line, which the name link's target spans; see *The labels*). Pointing at either opens the layer at once, also while another is open: its plate and label light, the scene suspends, and the layer counts as checked. The examples under a name, its vendor marks and names, are no trigger: pointing at them shows a mark's name (see *The marks' names*) and opens, switches, closes and checks nothing. The card shows once the pointer settles on one of the layer's triggers, that is, once the pointer has moved under 3px in the last 0.1s (a hovering pen drifts) or not at all for 0.12s, and a click on a plate shows it at once; a pointer that settles anywhere else shows no card. So a reader can point at every name, or every plate, in one continuous sweep, top to bottom or bottom to top, and check each layer in turn, with no card lying over the labels ahead: the pointer is read once a frame, and a card that has not shown covers nothing. A reader who stops on a name sees its card a moment later, placed clear of the label the sweep reaches next (see *Placement*). That label opens as the pointer reaches it, or, when the card lies past it, once the pointer stops on it or turns away from the card (see below), so a sweep that rests on a name, the first one included, goes on from there. A trigger that waits opens when the pointer turns away from the card or slows almost to a stop on it, or once the pointer has not moved towards the card for 200ms, so the pointer can cross the gap, the examples and the next label on its way to the card, however slowly, and stop there for less than 0.2s, without switching it, while a card that a passing pointer opened gives way at once. A name beyond the card, or a trigger off the way to it, opens at once. The way to a card above its label (T1 to T3, a card fitted over its label's head, or one past the label above) crosses no plate below it, so a sweep up and to the right on the plates never waits. A pointer that comes back over the edge of a plate it has just crossed opens the layer it left only once it is 8px (along x plus along y) from where it crossed, or rests for 200ms, so a pen hovering on the edge between two plates does not flicker between their layers. Keyboard focus on a name or on a card's stories link opens its card at once, and on a touch screen a tap on a plate or a row's chevron opens it at once and a second tap closes it; a tap or a click that focuses a name opens nothing through focus. The card closes 0.3s after a mouse or pen has left its layer's name, its plate and the card and stopped heading for it (leaving the stage included), unless keyboard focus is in its row; at once on Esc, when another layer's name or plate is chosen, when focus moves to the pause control, the links under the stage or elsewhere in the page, on a mouse or pen press outside the figure, and on a tap outside it. ### Scripts

`static/site.js` enhances pages that already work without it; every control it needs ships `hidden` or works as a plain link.

### Site assistant

The assistant ("Ask", 提问) is styled by `static/chat.css`, which the build links only when `chat.mode` in `content/site.json` is not `off`. It uses the tokens from `site.css` and nothing else, so dark mode needs no rules of its own; like the rest of the site it has no radius and no shadow. Every entry point ships `hidden` and `static/chat.js` reveals it, so a page without JavaScript shows no dead button.

- **Header launcher** (`chat.launcher: "header"`, the default). It sits in the header tools before search: a speech-square icon whose corner carries the seal, icon-only below 600px and a hairline-framed "Ask" button from 600px. While the panel is open the button fills graphite. A small square on its top-right corner marks an answer in progress (hollow) or an unread answer (filled), and the accessible name gains ", answering" or ", new answer". Below 400px the language switch drops its current-language label so the three tools keep 36px targets.
- **Float launcher** (`chat.launcher: "float"`). A graphite square at the bottom right with the seal in the accent, turning accent on hover. It is tucked away while the footer is in view and while the panel is open; the header button then stays hidden.
- **Article entry.** "Ask about this article" under a rule in the record column: one line of text and a full-width button with an accent arrow that inverts to graphite on hover. It opens the panel with three article questions (summary, what it does not show, sources) under the article's title.
- **Panel.** One `<dialog>` with three placements. Below 600px it is a modal full-screen sheet; from 600 to 959px a modal sheet from the right; from 960px a non-modal panel docked below the sticky header, a fixed-width column (`--chat-w`) with a 2px top rule, so the page stays readable and scrollable beside it. The sheets follow the visual viewport, so the on-screen keyboard never covers the composer. The head has a serif title, a "New chat" text button and the close button.
- **Status strip.** Under the head, one line (seal, state label, detail) that expands into the record of what was checked and what was only reported. A dashed edge means "this does not show", and solid fills mean evidence checked in this browser. It is never green and never shows a check mark.

| State | Strip | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `demo` | Hollow seal, dashed bottom edge | Answers assembled in this browser, or a mock backend that attests nothing |
| `pending` | The seal fills in steps | Fetching the backend's metadata and attestation |
| `reported` | Outline seal with a centre mark | The service describes itself, and this browser has not checked it |
| `partly_verified` | Half-filled seal | Some claims checked in this browser (planned for phase 2) |
| `verified` | Solid seal | Every check passed in this browser (planned); still no check mark |
| `failed` | Broken seal on the accent wash, heavy accent rule | A check failed, or the backend asks for an encryption this client does not support; nothing is sent |
| `unavailable`, `offline` | Struck-through seal on the tint | The backend cannot be reached or the browser is offline; the composer pauses, and the "Use demo answers instead" button appears when allowed |

- **Transcript.** A question is set in sans 600 behind a 2px left rule. An answer is a language island (serif in English, sans with looser leading in Chinese) that ends on the tombstone; while streaming the tombstone blinks as the cursor, and it turns hollow when an answer was stopped or failed. Notes such as a rate-limit countdown sit behind a hatch left rule, errors behind an accent rule.
- **Sources.** The article's source ledger in miniature, opened by a double rule: the accent `[n]` and the page title. The browser still compares each source's digest with the manifest, but the restyle layer hides the digest chips that `chat.js` adds, so answers print no digests.
- **Composer.** A textarea and a graphite Send button that becomes Stop, marked by an accent square, while an answer streams. The textarea keeps the 16px step so iOS does not zoom on focus, and it stays editable while the panel connects; only Send waits.
- **Chinese.** Labels are at least 13px and take the label tracking; the title uses the CJK serif; suggestions and the intro are set in sans with looser leading. Emphasis in a Chinese answer uses emphasis dots instead of italics.
- **Stacking.** The sticky header is `z-index: 40`, the float launcher 44, the docked panel 45 and the skip link 100. The modal sheets live in the browser's top layer, above all of them.
- **Forced colours and print.** Seals, digest marks and the tombstone use `CanvasText`, and the launchers and Send take a `ButtonText` frame. Print hides the panel, the launchers and the article entry.

**Construction.** A monoline V (a 2.6-unit stroke with a mitred point) on a 48-unit graphite tile, closed at the lower right by a seal-red square. The square carries three meanings: the tombstone that ends a proof, the dot in verifiable.pro, and a seal that authenticates a document. A check mark was rejected because it reads as a "verified" badge, which overclaims for a site whose own record says its digest does not establish that the reporting is correct. Outside the logo, only the site assistant still draws the seal.

**Wordmark.** "verifiable" in Newsreader 600 with ".pro" in a muted Newsreader italic. In the site header it sits on one line beside the mark, with no subtitle. The lockup files add "Verifiable AI Systems" in Plex Sans 500 beneath the wordmark.

**Sizes.**

- The master is used at 24px and up; below that, use the favicon cut.
- The header uses the mark at 32px on phones and 36px from 760px; the footer uses it at 32px.
- The lockup should be at least 120px wide.

**Clear space.** Keep a quarter of the tile (12 of 48 units) clear on every side.

**Do not:**

- recolour the seal or give the tile a second accent;
- round the tile's corners or add a shadow, gradient or outline;
- redraw the V as a check mark;
- set the wordmark in another face or move ".pro" out of its italic;
- place the dark tile on a dark ground (use the light-tile version);
- stretch the mark, or crop the square.

## Accessibility

- **Contrast.** All text pairs pass WCAG AA in both themes.
  - Metadata ink (`--ink-3`) stays above 4.5:1 on every panel, and TEE text reaches 7:1 on its wash.
  - The focus ring is a 2px outline in `--focus`. In the footer it switches to `--footer-focus`.
  - The large search field shows focus as a thicker accent underline.
- **Keyboard and enhancement.** Every control has a visible `:focus-visible` state. `[hidden]` is forced hidden, and the page works without JavaScript: the Latest and Resources filters ship hidden, every story and entry shows without them, and folds are native `<details>`.
- **Tags and marks.** Story tags are `aria-hidden` text with a visually hidden sentence, so they add no tab stops and read once. The stack's reach labels and the tiers' order note carry their meaning in words, not only in colour.
- **The hero.** The scene is `role="img"`, named by its title and described by its description, both in the page language; the figure has no caption, so the description also says what a caption would. Its labels are an ordered list with a name in the page language, after a visually hidden note that the logos and names are examples, not endorsements: each layer's code and name are a link, read as "T5, Agents" (a visually hidden comma between them), its icon is `aria-hidden`, a vendor mark is `role="img"` with the vendor's name (or `aria-hidden` beside the name it shows) and no tab stop, the TEE label reads as a sentence, and the leader lines are `aria-hidden`. The tooltip that shows a mark's name to a pointer, and the cards' names line on touch screens, are `aria-hidden`, since they repeat names that screen readers already hear; neither opens on CSS hover, and the tooltip takes no pointer events. The pause control has a visible label and a matching `aria-label`, and reduced motion stops the animation. The count of layers checked is a polite status (`role="status"`), so a screen reader hears each new count; a checked row's tick carries a visually hidden "Checked" (已查看); and "Check all layers" is a plain button with a visible label, which hands its focus to the pause control, or to the count under reduced motion, when it goes.
  - Each name link is described (`aria-describedby`) by its card's claim and "Who checks" lines, so a screen reader hears them wherever the card is not shown. The cards have no headings, so the page outline stays the `h1`, then "Latest news"; the person's icon and the legend's samples are `aria-hidden`, and a run of the legend reads as "In use: T1 to T2". The samples, which carry their meaning in their pattern as well as in the words beside them, keep 3:1 against the page in both themes (WCAG 1.4.11).
  - The tab order is "Check all layers" (the pause control once every layer is checked), then each layer's name and its card's stories link, T7 first, then the two links under the stage. There is no roving tab index and no arrow keys, and the vendor marks take no tab stop.
  - Esc closes the open card and, when focus was in it, moves focus to the row's name. That layer stays closed until the pointer leaves its name or plate or focus moves to another row.
  - Focus is never hidden or covered. `html { scroll-padding-top: var(--sticky-offset) }` keeps every focused element, and every anchor target, clear of the sticky header. Focusing a card's stories link opens the card; a card opened from the keyboard never covers its name; and keyboard focus brings the card and its label into view below the header, or the focused element alone when the two are taller than the window (`reveal()` in `hero.js`, which scrolls even by 0px, since any scroll from a focus handler replaces the browser's own scroll to where the element was). Focus on the pause control closes the card, and focus that the window gives back scrolls nothing.
  - On touch screens and phones each row has a chevron, a `button` with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` naming its card and a visually hidden name ("What can be checked at the Outputs layer"); on phones and touch tablets it is a 44px target, and on touch tablets nothing, not even its own card, lies over it. A card that a tap opens from 45em is scrolled wholly into view, never left under the sticky header or past the window's bottom.
  - No card opens on CSS hover (WCAG 1.4.13). A card opens only from a defined area, its layer's name area (the head line, at least 24px tall, with a pointer cursor and an underline under the mouse) or its plate; the vendor marks and names beside it only show their names. A card that the pointer opened is hoverable: it stays while the pointer is on its name, its plate or the card and while the pointer heads for it, the names and plates on the way waiting until the pointer rests on one for 0.2s or turns away, so the pointer can cross the gap, the examples and the next label to reach the card and its link. It is persistent until the pointer has left its name, plate and card for 0.3s, another layer is chosen, focus moves elsewhere or the reader presses outside, and dismissible with Esc. A pointer that sweeps the names or the plates checks every layer it crosses, and a card shows only once the pointer settles on one of its triggers, clear of the next label where the window allows, so a sweep goes on after a rest. Completing the checks takes no card away: the data starts moving beside the card that the last check opened, which stays open until it closes as any card does. Without JavaScript the cards are not displayed from 45em, where the name links still lead to the Stack page with their descriptions, and they are open in their rows on phones; the chevrons, the hint, the count of layers checked and "Check all layers" stay hidden, you are in full colour, and the scene's description says only what the still scene shows. When `hero.js` fails to load or throws before it sets the figure up, the page's guard gives you your colour back at the window's load, the description stays the still scene's, and the chevrons, the hint, the count and "Check all layers" stay hidden, as without the script.
  - The hint gives one line by input type (hover and pointer media features): pointing or Tab with a mouse or pen from 45em, a tap on the arrows on touch screens, a click on them with a mouse in the list below 45em. It is not a live region, so the line it swaps in once every layer is checked announces nothing beyond the count's own status.
  - While a layer is open, the other names step back to `--ink-3`, never to an opacity that fails contrast. A name, the legend or the hint that the shown card lies over is hidden instead (`.is-covered`, opacity 0), so no slice of it shows at the card's edge; a hidden name stays in the tab order, and focusing it opens its own card.
- **Assistant.** The launchers announce a dialog (`aria-haspopup="dialog"`). Opening moves focus to the question field with a fine pointer and to the panel title with a coarse one, so a phone keyboard does not cover the suggestions. Esc closes the panel and returns focus to the control that opened it, and a docked panel restored after navigation does not take focus. The transcript is a `role="log"` region, and progress is announced through a separate status line.
- **Motion.** `prefers-reduced-motion` removes transitions and smooth scrolling, shows the hero as a still frame (you regain colour at once as layers are checked), and runs the assistant's filling seal and blinking cursor only without it.
- **Forced colours.** Checked worksheet boxes, the search focus, the current section (underlined), the TEE label, the stack's TEE lines, the TEE tier and the hero's leader lines, label rules, icons and vendor marks use system colours. The hero's scene keeps its own paint, like an image. A layer card's frame, code chip and TEE line draw in `CanvasText`, the faded TEE line thinner, and the open row's rule and leader in `Highlight`; the legend's samples draw in `CanvasText` at full opacity, the ticks of checked layers in `CanvasText` and the border of "Check all layers" in `ButtonText`, and the tooltip that names a vendor mark keeps a `CanvasText` frame.
- **Mobile.** Tap targets in the header are at least 36px. There is no horizontal page scroll from 360 to 1600px on any page in either language. Safe-area insets are respected by the gutter and the sticky header.
- **Language.** Text in the other language carries its own `lang` attribute, and with it that language's type tokens and screen-reader voice: glossary equivalents and the language switch, English source, publisher and resource names on Chinese pages, and an English disclosure that is not yet translated.
- **Print.** Print hides the header, footer, filters, "Check or correct this page", the related row, "On this page" and the list copies of lead images (an article's lead figure prints with its caption and credit), and sets the page in ink on white, with the TEE tokens reset to ink and paper. The hero prints as a plain list: the person's label, the seven layers with their codes (T7 to T1), examples and "Who checks" lines, the legend (kept on one page) and the two links, without the scene, the leaders, the cards, the hint, the tooltip, the controls, the count of layers checked and the ticks. Print adds the URL of each source and each external link in the prose.

## The evidence chain and stack cards (21 October 2026, owner review)

- **Layers are positions, not security levels.** T1–T7 say where a claim sits, from the chip to policy: the laws, standards and agreements that govern the system. Layer 7 was called "Institutions & agreements" until 21 October 2026; its id and anchor stay `institutions`. A higher layer is not safer, and it builds on the layers below rather than containing them. The scheme is this site's own, not an industry standard; the tags note, the Stack page and the stack card say so, and so does the home hero's scene description (its caption did until 24 October 2026).
- **The evidence chain.** Each layer above T1 states in `content/topics.json` (`stack[].bind`) how its evidence is bound to the layer below, with a status: `deployed` (in use), `emerging` or `missing`. The Stack page's table draws a line through the layer codes: solid for deployed, dashed for emerging, two stubs and a gap for missing. The upper half of a row is the link from the layer above, the lower half the link to the layer below. The table also gives each binding in words, and a legend under it explains the line. The home page hero draws the same chain in its scene, at rest, with a legend of the chain as it is today (see [The hero](#the-hero)). A claim can be checked all the way up only while the chain is unbroken; where it breaks, checking stops.
- **Who checks.** Each layer also says in `content/topics.json` (`stack[].checkers`) who outside the provider can check its claim, and with what evidence. It shows in the layer's card on the home page, as the "Who checks" line under a row on phones (at rest on T1 and T2, and on the other rows once opened), and under each claim in the Stack page's table.
- **Stack cards.** A story's layer codes are a link to the first of its layers on the Stack page. On hover or keyboard focus, `site.js` opens a card with the whole stack from `#stack-data` and the story's layers marked. Touch screens, and pages without script, follow the link instead. The hero's layer cards are a different component (see [The hero](#the-hero)).
- **Home band.** The intro is the kicker ("Where your data goes and what protects it"), the tagline ("Verifiable AI needs evidence at all seven layers"), a one-sentence lead and two links; the editor line is in the footer. From a 70rem band (1184px) it is a 21rem column beside the hero; between 56 and 70rem it is a masthead over the hero, the kicker and tagline beside the rest; below that it is one column over the hero. "Latest news" follows: the lead brief with its dek, and four more briefs beside it. The home page has no stack table.
